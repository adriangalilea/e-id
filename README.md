# e-ID

Your digital identity: one page at [👤.to/you](https://👤.to) for everywhere you are online. Sign in with GitHub or Google and it fills itself.

## What it does

- **One address, three spellings.** `e-id.to/you`, `eid.to/you` and `👤.to/you` are the same page. Safari shows the emoji in its address bar, so Safari is sent to `👤.to` and every other browser to `e-id.to`; signing in on one is signed in on the other.
- **Links by name, on every card.** Nothing to set up: each link on your card is at its platform's names, `/you/github` or `/you/gh`, `x` (`twitter`, `tw`), `youtube` (`yt`), `instagram` (`ig`), `telegram` (`tg`), `email` (`mail`), `website` (`web`, `site`). The names are one table, `LINK_NAMES` in `lib/link-names`.
- **The card as data.** `/you/card.json` (or `/you/json`) in an open format ([its schema](https://e-id.to/card.schema.json)), `/you/card.vcf` (or `/you.vcf`) for contacts, `/you/card.svg` and `/you/byline.svg` as images, and `curl eid.to/you` prints it as text (`/you/txt`).
- **Short links.** `/you/<name>` to anywhere, kept in their own list, each counting its opens and, if you like, stopping on a day or after a number of opens.
- **Latest.** Your page opens on what you made lately, read from the links you already have: repositories you pushed to, posts on your website's feed, uploads to your channel, newest first.
- **Embeds.** `<e-id-card handle="you">` on any page with one script (`/embed.js`), and oEmbed, so an address pasted into a site that reads it becomes the card.
- **Vouches.** Visitors leave a word on your page; you pin the one you want shown, or turn vouches off.
- **Your numbers.** How many visits your page had and how many of your links were opened, under your card, counted without cookies.
- **Checked links.** Every address you point somewhere of your choosing is checked with Google Safe Browsing, and every link has a preview at `/you/<name>+`: whose it is and where it goes, before anyone opens it.
- **A CLI.** `npx @e-id/eid link <url>` from any terminal, nothing installed: short links, your numbers, anyone's card. The first command signs the machine in (a code, approved with GitHub or Google at `/cli`, where its key is revocable); installed (`npm i -g @e-id/eid`) it answers to `eid` and to `👤`, and `eid skill install` gives agents a skill that never falls behind it.

Built with Next.js, better-auth, and Drizzle on Turso. The interface comes from [ui.adriangalilea.com](https://ui.adriangalilea.com).

## Decisions

- **The person is the namespace.** `eid.to/you` is the product and stays at the root, never under `/u/`. Everything a person makes lives under their name, `eid.to/you/<name>`: a platform (`/you/gh`), a link of theirs (`/you/cv`), a short-lived share. Whoever receives one sees whose it is before opening it, and every link belongs to a signed-in account, so abuse is banned with the account.
- **One rule for `/you/<name>`:** a platform name opens that link, a name of theirs opens that link, anything else (a typo, a platform they have no link on) opens their profile. Only a person who does not exist is a 404.
- **No anonymous short links on these domains,** at the root, under a path or on a subdomain. They are what phishing runs on, and once enough bad ones pass through, Safe Browsing and mail filters flag the whole domain, every profile with it. Anonymous shortening, if ever, gets a domain of its own.
- **e-ID's own names are reserved** in `forbiddenUsernames` (`api`, `dev`, and what comes). Usernames are 5 characters or more today; whether shorter ones are sold is open (Premium).
- **A card shows where you are; a short link is what you hand out.** Two things, one table (`link`, platform `link` for your own). The card holds your platforms and at most 12 marks in all (about two rows), edited on the card. Short links, `/you/<name>` to anywhere (a CV, a talk, a file), are kept in their own list (the "short links" button beside share and edit), up to 200 once the account has earned them (below), each with its opens counted and an optional end (a day, a number of opens, 1 being one use). A short link can also be on the card, named there; one that stops opening never is, since the card is cached and could not know when. Named by you or a made-up code (six letters and digits, always a digit, so never a word or a platform's name); a platform's names and the card's files are refused (`refusedLinkName`). A crawler (a link preview) is sent on without using an open, or pasting a one-use link anywhere would spend it.
- **"Latest" is read, never set up** (`lib/activity`): your newest uploads (a YouTube channel's feed), the repositories you pushed to last (GitHub), and your posts (the RSS or Atom feed your website names, `<link rel="alternate">`), each drawn whole by its own card (a repo with its commits, a post's preview, a video's player). Ordered by date and nothing else, newest first: the five newest from the last 12 months, whatever their source. Older than a year is no news: an upload from 2006 would lead the page.
- **e-ID's links are kept from leading to phishing or malware**, as far as Google Safe Browsing knows (it can miss a bad page or flag a good one, and every place that claims the protection says so). Every address a person points somewhere of their choosing (their website, a link of their own, on the card or a short link) is asked when it is saved (`lib/link-safety`), every day after (`app/api/cron/recheck`, Vercel's cron, the links asked longest ago first), and when its preview is shown. One listed is refused, or taken down: it opens nothing and leaves the card. `/admin` lists the newest links with a switch to take one down or put it back. What a person is told follows Google's terms for its data (`lib/threats`): a warning in qualified words ("may be"), a link to more about that threat, "Advisory provided by Google", and only on an answer minutes old, so a link taken down days ago is said to be taken down by e-ID, not to be phishing. Safe Browsing's terms are non-commercial, which e-ID is while it takes no money; the day it does, the same lists move to Web Risk (Premium › billing, below).
- **Links of your own are earned, not given** (`lib/trust`): an account costs nothing to make, so a new one keeps 1, 10 after a week, 50 after a month, the full 200 after three months. A GitHub account a year old counts a week older. A link taken down freezes the account at what it has until it is put back. The short links list says the limit when it holds you back, and when it grows.
- **Every link has a preview at `/you/<name>+`** (bit.ly's convention, `proxy.ts`): whose it is (their card, laid out as their profile is), where it goes, whether it still opens, what Safe Browsing says of it now, and the way on unless it warns. No open is used: the way on is a form posted to the link, which counts the open there.
- **A link e-ID is not sure of opens its preview first** (`lib/trust` `unsureOf`): one from an account in its first week, the week a throwaway account made for one phishing link is used, or one whose destination Safe Browsing has never answered for. The preview says why it was shown, and a pasted address cannot skip it (the way on is a form, never a link). A chat's link preview reads that page too, so a stranger's new link shows e-ID's page, not the destination's.
- **The domain you hand out is chosen where you copy it** (`lib/addresses`): `👤.to` in a post or a message (X and GitHub show it as written), `xn--mq8h.to` (the same domain spelled out) for a field that refuses the emoji but shows it back (X's website field), `eid.to` to say or type, `e-id.to` where an emoji is refused for good. One choice, remembered, the same in the share menu and the short links; the card's own address is always `👤.to`, as it shows. A copy is always the whole address: `👤.to/you` without `https://` is a link nowhere it was checked, with it everywhere. The share menu's X row shows the spelled-out address in X's own field and the profile it becomes (ui `x-profile`).
- **The CLI is a client of the API, and the API is the site's own path** (`cli/`, `app/api/v1`, `lib/api-keys`). `eid` signs a machine in with the OAuth device flow (RFC 8628, as `gh` does): a code in the terminal, approved signed in at `/cli` (an account made there if there is none, its address picked in the same press), and a key back to the CLI that asked (`eid_…`, kept hashed, named after the machine, listed and revocable at `/cli`). It works over ssh, and the key never passes through a URL. The writes take a user, not a session (`lib/short-links`, `lib/card-write`), so the site and the API are one path with every limit in it; the card is changed one thing at a time (`PATCH /card`, `PUT`/`DELETE /card/links/:platform`), never replaced whole (a script replacing everything is easy to get wrong). The commands are one table (`cli/src/commands.ts`) that the help, the agent skill and the dispatcher all read; the skill is printed by the installed `eid` and rewritten by every newer one. Released from CI by npm's trusted publishing (`.github/workflows/cli-release.yml` on adriangalilea/ci), tagged `cli-v…`; its version is `cli/package.json`'s alone, bundled in.
- **Every shape the API speaks is one schema** (`lib/api-schemas`): a route parses its body by it and its answer by it before it leaves, so a route drifting from what is published throws on the server, never in a client; the OpenAPI file is generated from the same schemas (`/api/v1/openapi.json`); the CLI's `--json` prints the API's answer as it came, typed by them; and a refusal is one shape everywhere, `{ error: { code, message, fields? } }`, on the API and on the CLI's stdout (exit 1), so a program acts on `code`, never on words. Per key, 60 writes a minute (each server's memory) and 1000 a day (counted in the database, one statement per write: `api_key_day`).
- **Sending a file is a link of its own, served from its owner's machine** (`lib/sends`, `cli/src/sendme.ts`). `npx @e-id/eid send notes.zip` prints `npx @e-id/eid get eid.to/you/notes`, which anyone runs with nothing installed and no account; a browser opening the name gets a page with that command. The file goes from machine to machine, never through e-ID, which holds only the name, the file's name and size, and the ticket, until the sender stops (the name goes with it). n0's [`sendme`](https://github.com/n0-computer/sendme) does the moving, not a protocol of ours: the CLI fetches its release build for the machine, pinned to one version and checked against the SHA-256 GitHub published for it, and runs it. n0's public relays pass the traffic where the two machines cannot reach each other: they see who talks to whom, never the file. n0 says those are for development and testing (rate-limited, no guarantees), which fits sends while they are few; before sends see real use they move to n0's paid shared relays or a relay of our own (one admitting e-ID's senders, which their receivers then reach; to do, below). Where the machines connect directly each sees the other's IP address, and `eid send`, the skill and the privacy page all say so. A send shares the person's one namespace (`link.kind` `send`, the opens, the takedown, the allowance), is never on the card and never asked of Safe Browsing (its value is a ticket, not an address), and has no price (x402, below).
- **The components come from the @ag registry, and what of it is not free is committed sealed** (`components/ui`, `scripts/sealed.mts`, ui's `@ag/sealed`). A file opening with an @ag paid or studio item's line is kept out of git and committed encrypted beside itself (`<file>.sealed`, AES-256-GCM); `pnpm build` opens it with `SEALED_KEY` (Vercel, and 1Password's "sealed key"), `pnpm check` fails when a sealed copy is stale or the file is tracked, and `pnpm seal` re-seals after `mise add` or an edit. The list of what to seal is read off the files, never kept. Today that is the home's shimmer (a studio item) and the cards for posts and profiles on X, GitHub, Telegram and YouTube (`tweet`, `x-profile`, `github`, `social-profile`, paid items). A clone without the key cannot build the home: those files are not this repository's source (ATTRIBUTIONS).
- **Patterns live on `/dev`** (the dev server, or an admin): each thing done in more than one place, drawn by the components that do it, with the facts it stands on (where each domain becomes a link, and how that was checked). A new place doing the same thing takes the pattern.
- **A page anyone can make e-ID fetch asks the public internet only.** The home's live preview reads the websites a visitor types: through ui's `public-fetch`, which checks the address each connection actually uses (no internal address, no DNS rebinding), and at most 20 previews a visitor every 10 minutes (`lib/rate-limit`, in each server's memory, nothing stored).
- **Keep only what is used.** A session is a token and its dates (never an IP address or a browser); a provider's tokens are read once, at linking, then cleared; visits are counted without cookies and never store an IP address. The [privacy page](https://e-id.to/privacy) says what is kept, and must change when the code does.
- **Deleting an account takes everything of theirs at once**, numbers included (`forgetPerson`). Their names are held from anyone else for 90 days (`released_name`), so links still out there open nobody else. The vouches they wrote on other pages stay, signed "a former e-ID member": the page's owner keeps them, nothing of the writer does.

  3. **A send for a browser.** Today a send's page shows the `npx` command; a browser getting the file itself waits on whether `iroh-blobs` runs in one (iroh does, over the relay; unchecked).
  4. **Built: sending to people, an inbox, and asking before taking** (lib/sends, `send_to`; CLI 0.2.1):
     - `eid send <path> --to ada,bob`: addressed to e-ID accounts, not public. No page, no name anyone else opens: the API hands the ticket only to the named people's keys, and to anyone else answers the not found of a stopped send.
     - `eid inbox`: what is waiting, each with who sent it (their name and `@handle`), the file's name and size, and since when.
     - `eid inbox --watch` (`-w`): waits for arrivals into the current folder, as `eid get` does (`-d`/`--dir`, chosen as the watch begins), shows each sender (with a link to their page) before anything comes down, asks first (y/N), and goes on until Ctrl-C; `--once` (`-o`, so `-wo`) ends it at the first file taken, the contract of the house's other watchers. For a program, `--json` streams arrivals as lines and takes nothing (`-wo --json`: the first, then exit); `eid get <address>` takes one.
     - **The answer reaches the sender** (CLI 0.2.6): looking at a send counts nothing; the answer does (`POST /sends/:u/:n/answer`, yes or no), and `eid send` prints it live: said yes and downloading, got it, said no. A no takes the send out of that person's inbox. A send `--to` people stops by itself once each has taken it or said no (`--stay` keeps it); a send for anyone runs until Ctrl-C. `eid get` shows a progress bar, read off what sendme keeps as it fetches.
     - **To yourself:** `eid send file -s` (`--self`, combinable with `-t ada`) waits in your own inbox, for your other machines.
     - Asking first, everywhere a file arrives: `eid get` shows the sender and the file and asks in a terminal; a program says yes for the one file it chose with `--yes`, and without a terminal is refused without it. Taking without asking only by person, `--accept-from ada` (who, never "anything"): a blanket accept-all is how a stranger's file lands unread.
     - `eid send --password`: a public send whose ticket is released only for the password (scrypt-hashed at e-ID, checked there, said in the `e-id-password` header, never the address), for someone without an account.
     - **Whether the sender's machine is there** (CLI 0.2.5): each question `eid send` asks for its events is its machine heard from (`link.seen_at`). Unheard for a minute, the send is paused: no ticket is given, its page and `eid inbox` say so, and `eid get` waits for it. Heard again, it is live. Unheard for a day, the daily sweep (`/api/cron/sends`) deletes it and frees the name.
     - **The same address for the same file:** sending a file again from the same machine takes the name it had (`sent.json` in the CLI's config, by path), unless a name is given or that one is taken (the API says `taken`).
     - **A dropped download is picked up:** sendme keeps what came in the folder (`.sendme-recv-<hash>`, by the file's content) and fetches only the rest, so `eid get` asks for the send again (a look, which counts nothing) and goes on from whatever ticket it has now, a sender back with a new one included. Eight drops in one run and it stops, saying what came is kept.
     - The outbox is `eid ls`: each send with whom it is for and whether each took it or said no. Taken means the file came (`POST /sends/:u/:n/taken`, after the download), never that the ticket was given.
     - The inbox learns by asking: the CLI holds one request open (`GET /inbox?wait=25`, answered within a second and a half of an arrival), so nothing runs on e-ID's side to keep connections.
     - What it is not: a sendme ticket is a bearer capability (whoever holds it fetches the file while it is served); e-ID decides who is given it, nothing more.
  5. **`eid upload`: a kept file, hosted, Premium.** Its own verb, never a flag on `send`: `send` goes from this machine to theirs and e-ID never sees the file, `upload` gives the file to e-ID, which then holds it, sees it and answers for it (the DSA, below). The same addresses, `--to` and `--password` as a send, the file outliving the laptop: stored on R2 (no charge for downloads, which is a file link's whole cost), scanned, checked and taken down like any link. Live and free while the sender is online, kept and paid when it must outlast them.
     - **DEFERRED, deliberately (2026-10-17): a legal and business decision before a coding one.** Peer-to-peer sends already share a file without e-ID ever holding it; hosting makes e-ID the operator of other people's files, with the DSA's host duties (below). The precedent to remember: Mozilla shut Firefox Send down in 2020, a send-a-file-by-link service, because it became a malware and phishing host. There is no Safe Browsing for files; safety is layers, and none is optional:
       - **Child abuse material (the legal must-have):** Cloudflare's CSAM Scanning Tool (free to Cloudflare customers, fuzzy hashes against NCMEC's lists, for content served through Cloudflare's cache, which a public R2 bucket on our domain is); alternatives Microsoft PhotoDNA, Thorn's Safer.
       - **Malware:** ClamAV (self-hosted, signature-based, the baseline) and a hash lookup on VirusTotal, whose public API forbids commercial use (the paid tier allows it).
       - **The design, which is the cheapest protection:** files served from a separate domain (as Google serves `googleusercontent.com`), so one flagged file can never get `eid.to` or `e-id.to` blocklisted; private by default (for `--to` people only, no public link); a short retention; executables refused; quotas by account age (`lib/trust`); a report button, and a hash blocklist on every takedown so the same file cannot come back.
       - **Build order if it is ever built:** private-first with every layer above in place before the first upload, never "add scanning later".
  6. **Payments, x402.** Two angles, both binding:
     - *Product.* [x402](https://www.x402.org) (Coinbase's open protocol) answers a request with HTTP 402 and what to pay; the payer's client pays (a stablecoin, on chain, through a facilitator that verifies and settles) and asks again with the proof. e-ID only says what to pay and to whom: the payment goes from the payer's wallet straight to the owner's (`payTo`), e-ID never holds money, never moves it, takes no cut. Start with `eid.to/you/pay` (x402 for agents, a page for people; a person still needs a wallet), then a price on what e-ID can see: a kept file (5), or a link to the owner's own site. Taking no cut keeps e-ID non-commercial for Safe Browsing; Premium does not (billing, below).
     - *Law and abuse.* **No payment on peer-to-peer content.** Paid, unseen by e-ID and hosted by nobody is exactly what illegal content sells on, and every such link carries e-ID's domains: the flagging this repo works to avoid (Decisions), and liability with it. As a host (the cards; kept files) e-ID is under the EU's Digital Services Act: a notice-and-action mechanism (art. 16), reasons for every takedown (art. 17), a contact point (arts. 11 and 12), and acting quickly once told; content it cannot see is content it cannot answer for. On money: e-ID neither holds nor moves funds, which is the line between software and a payment or crypto-asset service (PSD2 in the EU; MiCA for crypto-asset services; in the US, FinCEN's 2019 guidance treats non-custodial software as no money transmitter). Whether that line holds for e-ID as built is a lawyer's question, asked before anything takes money for content: the DSA's duties at e-ID's size, MiCA and Spain's anti-money-laundering rules for a non-custodial checkout, consumer law for what is sold, and the terms of service that say who answers for what.

### Premium

**The line** (decided; prices are the plan, tweakable): free is everything that makes e-ID spread and costs it nearly nothing per person; Pro is what costs money per person, what is scarce, and what only heavy use needs. Never behind it: the checks that keep links safe, privacy, and getting a file someone sends you (every `npx @e-id/eid get` is someone meeting e-ID). Said publicly at [/pro](https://e-id.to/pro), which also counts who wants it (`pro_interest`, `/admin`): the number that says when billing is worth building.

- **Free, always:** the card and page on every domain, links by name, the card as contact, data and embed, short links (200, earned), link checks and previews, live sends and getting files, the inbox when it comes, the CLI and the API at 60 writes a minute and 1000 a day (gate volume, never access: a script brings people, and a call costs a row).
- **Pro, about €4 a month or €36 a year:** kept files, 10 GB (the anchor: a real cost and plain value); your own domain for your card; your numbers in full (built, `readOwner`, admins only today); no waiting (the earned allowance skipped, payment being its own proof of a person) and higher API limits; `you@eid.to`.
- **Once, not a subscription:** short usernames (3 and 4 characters) and more usernames.
- **What it costs, once anyone pays** (about $40 to $60 a month, so some 15 to 25 subscribers; estimates): Vercel Pro ($20 a month: Hobby is "non-commercial, personal use only"), Web Risk for link checks, relays for sends (to do, above), the three `.to` domains, forwarding for `you@eid.to`. Turso's free tier holds for a long while.
- **The order:** now, everything free and `/pro` counting; when enough ask, or when kept files are built, whichever is first, billing opens with kept files as its anchor; then usernames sold once.

- [ ] billing: **decided:** Stripe Managed Payments, Stripe's merchant-of-record mode, on the owner's existing Stripe account. Stripe is the seller of record: it charges and remits VAT in every country, issues the receipts, and carries the EU's 14-day withdrawal right, so e-ID gets payouts, never a VAT registration of its own (OSS) or an invoice a sale. **Sold by the company that will own e-ID, and nothing charged before it exists:** e-ID moves to it, which also makes it the controller the privacy page names. Left to decide: whether a subscription needs anything a one-off sale did not (the company's side is asked), and what a lapsed payment takes away (kept files kept 30 days, then gone, said before). **The day before the first payment, flip every switch that taking money flips:**
  - the company owns e-ID, and the privacy page names it as controller, users told
  - Vercel Pro (Hobby is non-commercial only)
  - link checking to Web Risk: Safe Browsing is for non-commercial use only; Web Risk is the same lists under a commercial licence, a billing account on Google Cloud, `lib/safe-browsing` and its key, nothing else
  - sends on relays fit for production (to do, above)
  - the terms (a subscription, its renewal, withdrawal) and the privacy page (Stripe among who else handles data)
  - write to everyone `/pro` counted, once (they were told so)
- [ ] analytics in full for Pro: built (`readOwner`), shown to admins only today; everyone keeps their totals.
- [ ] your own domain for your card: a CNAME to e-ID, the domain added to the project through Vercel's domains API, the card answered at it.
- [ ] more usernames, sold: each a permanent redirect to your page, so a new main name keeps every old link working. The redirect is built (`username_alias`, `adriangalilea` → `adrian`); buying one, and renaming yourself with the old name kept, are not.
- [ ] claiming a squatted username: a request to take an address someone holds and does not use, or that is your own name or brand (the terms already allow taking one back); decide who judges and how long an unused one is held
- [ ] short usernames (3 and 4 characters) sold, 1 and 2 kept for e-ID; decide the price, and whether a bought name can be resold
- [ ] an email address, `you@eid.to`
  - Why: `eid.to/you/mail` opens your email link in a browser, but a mail client's "To:" refuses it (an address is `name@domain`, never a path). A real address reaches you without showing your own.
  - Gated: every alias is a relay anyone can write to, and forwarding costs money per domain.
  - Inbound mail on `eid.to`, `e-id.to` and `👤.to` (`xn--mq8h.to`) to a forwarding service that rewrites the sender so forwarded mail passes the receiving server's checks (SRS) and filters spam: [Forward Email](https://forwardemail.net) (open source, alias API, about $3 a month a domain). Not Cloudflare Email Routing: it forwards only to addresses verified inside the account.
  - e-ID keeps the aliases: setting, changing or removing the email link creates, moves or deletes `you@eid.to` through the service's API (its key in the deployment's environment, never here).
  - Decide: the address shown (`eid.to`, the shortest, or `e-id.to`), and whether it needs a public email link or is a private alias for any member.
  - `e-id.to` forwards a few fixed aliases today: list them before its mail moves, so none is lost.
